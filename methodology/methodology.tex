\fronttitle{ABSTRACT}
\justifying

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems rely on effective retrieval of external information to support language models in answering questions that may require knowledge beyond their internal parameters. While conventional retrieval methods primarily focus on the relevance of documents to the original query, this objective becomes less sufficient in iterative retrieval settings, where some of the required information may already have been collected, and subsequent retrieval needs to focus on what is still missing. In such settings, repeatedly retrieving documents that are similar to previously obtained evidence can lead to redundancy and limit the usefulness of later retrieval steps.

This project explores \textbf{State Aware Retrieval}, a retrieval approach developed for iterative information retrieval. The approach considers not only the relevance of a document to the question, but also the extent to which it is related to information that is currently missing and to evidence that has already been collected. These signals are used to guide retrieval toward documents that can provide useful complementary information.

The proposed approach is integrated into the MIGRES framework, where missing information is identified during the retrieval process and used to guide subsequent information seeking. State Aware Retrieval focuses on the document selection step, with the broader objective of making iterative retrieval more targeted, less redundant, and better aligned with the information that remains necessary for answering a question.

\vspace{0.10in}
\textbf{Keywords:} Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Information Retrieval,  Iterative Retrieval, Multi-Hop Question Answering.

\chaptertitle{1}{INTRODUCTION}
\justifying

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that combines information retrieval with large language models to improve question answering. Instead of depending only on the information stored in a language model, RAG retrieves relevant information from external documents and uses it to generate an answer \cite{lewis2020rag}. This makes RAG useful for tasks where the required information may be spread across different documents.

However, answering complex questions is not always as simple as finding documents that are related to the question. Some questions require multiple pieces of information, and the first retrieval step may provide only part of what is needed. Multi-hop question answering benchmarks and iterative retrieval methods show that solving such questions can require evidence from multiple passages or documents and repeated retrieval steps \cite{yang2018hotpotqa,trivedi2022musique,yadav2020iterative}. If the retrieval system continues to select documents mainly because they are related to the original question, it may repeatedly retrieve similar information instead of finding the missing evidence.

MIGRES (Missing Information Guided Retrieve-Extraction-Solving) provides a framework for handling this type of iterative information retrieval. It keeps track of the information already collected and identifies information that is still required to answer the question \cite{wang2025migres}. This creates an opportunity to improve the retrieval stage by considering not only the original question, but also the current information gap and the evidence already collected. Related work on relevant information gain also motivates reducing redundant evidence while preserving useful information for RAG \cite{pickett2024better}.

This project proposes a State Aware Retrieval for improving document selection in such iterative RAG systems. The main idea is to select documents based on their relevance to the question, their usefulness for covering the missing information, and the amount of new information they can provide compared with the evidence already collected.

The proposed approach will be studied within the MIGRES framework and evaluated on question-answering datasets. It will be compared with existing retrieval approaches to examine its effect on retrieval quality, answer quality, and retrieval efficiency. The study will also investigate the importance of the different factors considered by the proposed approach.

The overall aim of this project is to explore whether making the retrieval process aware of the current information gap can help RAG systems retrieve more useful and complementary evidence for complex questions.

\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{3. PROBLEM DEFINITION}
\chaptertitle{3}{PROBLEM DEFINITION}
\justifying

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=0.30in,itemsep=0.12in,parsep=0pt]

\item
In iterative Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), some useful information may already be retrieved in earlier steps. However, the next retrieval may return similar information instead of focusing on what is still missing.

\item
In conventional retrieval, documents are mainly selected according to their relevance to the query. This may favor information that has already been collected instead of information that is still missing.

\item
Previous evidence is not fully considered when selecting documents for later retrieval. As a result, redundant documents may be selected while useful and complementary documents may be missed.

\item
Therefore, there is a need for a state aware retrieval approach that considers the query, missing information, and previously collected evidence to select more useful documents for the next retrieval step.

\end{enumerate}

\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{5. SOLUTION STRATEGY / METHODOLOGY}

\chaptertitle{5}{SOLUTION STRATEGY / METHODOLOGY}

\justifying

The proposed methodology introduces \textbf{State Aware Retrieval} as a direct retrieval algorithm within the MIGRES framework \cite{wang2025migres}. The main reasoning, evidence extraction, and information gap update steps of MIGRES remain unchanged. Only the document retrieval step is replaced by the proposed method.

At each iteration $t$, the method uses the original question $q$, the collected evidence $E_t$, the current information gaps $G_t$, and the document corpus $\mathcal{D}$. The documents are embedded using a fixed Sentence Transformer model, and the embeddings are stored for reuse. The question, gaps, and evidence are also converted into embeddings.

Each document is compared with the question, all current gaps, and the collected evidence using semantic similarity. These similarity values are then combined to give a final state aware score for each document. The exact scoring equation will be finalized during implementation and experimentation.

The documents are ranked by their final scores, and the top-$K$ documents are selected. The value of $K$ can be set by the user for each retrieval setting.

The methodology therefore follows five main stages:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=0.30in,itemsep=0.14in,parsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Corpus embedding:} encode the complete document corpus using the fixed Sentence Transformer model and store the document embeddings for reuse. When new documents are added, encode their embeddings and add them to the stored corpus.

\item \textbf{MIGRES state preparation:} use the MIGRES framework to maintain the question, collected evidence, and current missing information during the iterative process.

\item \textbf{State aware document scoring:} compare each document in the corpus with the question, each current information-gap requirement, and the evidence collected so far.

\item \textbf{Top-$K$ retrieval:} use the resulting state aware score to rank the corpus documents and retrieve the highest-scoring $K$ documents.

\item \textbf{Iterative state update:} pass the selected documents to MIGRES for filtering and evidence extraction, update the evidence and information-gap state, and repeat the retrieval process when more information is required.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proposed Solution}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

The main problem is that in iterative retrieval, a document may be relevant to the original question but may not provide the information that is still missing. It may also contain information that has already been collected. This can lead to repeated retrieval and less useful evidence \cite{yadav2020iterative,pickett2024better,wang2025infogain}.

To address this problem, we propose \textbf{State Aware Retrieval}, a direct retrieval method that uses the current retrieval state when selecting documents. The current state includes the original question $q$, the information gaps $G_t$, and the evidence collected so far $E_t$. Instead of selecting documents only by their similarity to the original question, the proposed method considers all three parts of the current state.

The complete document corpus $\mathcal{D}$ is used as the searchable corpus. The documents are represented using a fixed Sentence Transformer model. At each iteration, the question, current gaps, and collected evidence are also represented in the same semantic space. Each document is compared with these state elements using semantic similarity.

The resulting similarity information is used by the State Aware Retrieval method to assign a final score to each document. The documents are ranked using this score, and the top-$K$ documents are selected for retrieval.

The selected documents are then passed to MIGRES for its existing filtering, evidence extraction, and information gap update process. The updated state is used in the next retrieval iteration. In this way, the proposed method changes its retrieval decisions as the available information changes.

\section{Mathematical Formulation}

At iteration $t$, the retrieval state contains the question $q$, the collected evidence $E_t$, the current information gap $G_t$, and the complete corpus $\mathcal{D}$. The retrieval method uses this state to assign one final score to every document in the corpus.

\subsection{Notation}

Let
\[
E_t=\{e_1,e_2,\ldots,e_n\}
\]
be the evidence collected before iteration $t$, and let
\[
G_t=\{g_1,g_2,\ldots,g_m\}
\]
be the information gaps identified by MIGRES. The searchable document corpus is:
\[
\mathcal{D}=\{d_1,d_2,\ldots,d_N\}.
\]

The current retrieval state is represented as:
\[
\boxed{(q,E_t,G_t,\mathcal{D})}.
\]

\subsection{Corpus Embedding}

Before retrieval begins, all documents in the corpus are encoded using the selected Sentence Transformer model. The resulting document embeddings are stored and reused during retrieval:
\[
\phi(d) \leftarrow \text{Sentence Transformer}(d).
\]

At the end of each retrieval iteration, the system checks for new or updated documents in the corpus. New documents are encoded using the same model and added to the stored embeddings. Updated documents are re-encoded and replaced with their new embeddings. This keeps the searchable embedding collection up to date without re-embedding unchanged documents.

\subsection{State Embedding}

At each iteration, the question, current gaps, and collected evidence are encoded using the same Sentence Transformer:
\[
\phi(q),\qquad \phi(g_i),\qquad \phi(e_j).
\]

This places the question, gaps, evidence, and documents in the same semantic space.

\subsection{Sentence Similarity}

The semantic similarity between two text items $x$ and $y$ is measured using cosine similarity:
\[
\operatorname{cos}(x,y)=
\frac{\phi(x)^T\phi(y)}{\|\phi(x)\|_2\|\phi(y)\|_2}.
\]

For the proposed scoring method, the value can be normalized to the range $[0,1]$ using:
\[
\boxed{
 s(x,y)=\frac{1+\operatorname{cos}(x,y)}{2}
}
\tag{1}
\]

A higher value means that the two representations are more semantically similar.

\subsection{Question Relevance}

Question relevance measures how closely a document matches the original question:
\[
\boxed{R(d,q)=s(d,q)}
\tag{2}
\]

A higher value means that the document is more related to the main question.

\subsection{Gap Coverage}

The document is compared with every current information gap:
\[
\boxed{c(d,g_i)=s(d,g_i)}
\tag{3}
\]

The gap coverage score combines the similarity values for the current gaps:
\[
\boxed{
C(d,G_t)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}c(d,g_i)
}
\tag{4}
\]

A higher value means that the document is more closely related to the information that is currently missing. At the first iteration, $G_0=\emptyset$ and the gap-coverage value is defined as $C(d,G_0)=0$. If no valid gap remains after a completed iteration, MIGRES can stop the retrieval process.

\subsection{Evidence Novelty}

The document is compared with all previously collected evidence. The novelty score is based on the document's strongest similarity with existing evidence:
\[
\boxed{
N(d,E_t)=1-\max_{e_j\in E_t}s(d,e_j)
}
\tag{5}
\]

A document that is very similar to previous evidence receives a lower novelty value. A document that is less similar to the existing evidence receives a higher novelty value. At the first iteration, when no evidence has been collected:
\[
\boxed{N(d,E_0)=1.}
\tag{6}
\]

\subsection{State Aware Document Score}

The three signals are combined to give one final score to every document:
\[
\boxed{
S_t(d\mid q,E_t,G_t)=
\alpha R(d,q)+\beta C(d,G_t)+\gamma N(d,E_t)
}
\tag{7}
\]

where:
\[
\boxed{
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq0,
\qquad
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Here, $\alpha$ controls the importance of question relevance, $\beta$ controls the importance of current gap coverage, and $\gamma$ controls the importance of new information compared with previous evidence.

\subsection{Top-$K$ Retrieval}

After the final score is calculated for the documents in the corpus, they are ranked from highest to lowest score. The final retrieval result is:
\[
\boxed{
T_t=\operatorname{TopK}_{d\in\mathcal{D}} S_t(d\mid q,E_t,G_t)
}
\tag{9}
\]

The parameter $K$ defines how many documents are returned. No fixed similarity threshold is used before Top-$K$ selection.

\subsection{Iterative State Update}

The selected documents $T_t$ are passed to MIGRES for filtering and information extraction. The useful extracted information is added to the evidence state, and MIGRES identifies the remaining gap for the next iteration:
\[
\boxed{
(q,E_t,G_t,\mathcal{D})
\rightarrow
T_t
\rightarrow
(E_{t+1},G_{t+1})
\rightarrow
T_{t+1}
}
\tag{10}
\]

The process continues until MIGRES determines that enough information has been collected or the configured iteration limit is reached.

The evaluation uses six question-answering datasets used in the MIGRES setting. They cover multi-hop reasoning, information gathering, and evidence-based question answering:

    \item \textbf{WikiHop:} A multi-document question-answering dataset requiring information from multiple documents \cite{welbl2018wikihop}.

    \item \textbf{StrategyQA:} A question-answering dataset requiring implicit reasoning over supporting information \cite{geva2021strategyqa}.
\end{itemize}

Official training, development, and test splits are kept separate wherever available. Validation data are used for system development and parameter selection, while the final test data are kept for the final comparison.

\subsection{Model}

\texttt{sentence-transformers/multi-qa-mpnet-base-cos-v1} model.
It is a Sentence Transformer model designed for semantic search. It
converts text into 768-dimensional dense vectors. The same model is
used for questions, documents, information gaps, and evidence
\cite{reimers2019sentence,st2020mpnet}.

The document corpus is embedded before retrieval and the embeddings are
stored for reuse. At each iteration, the current question, gaps, and
evidence are embedded using the same model. The resulting vectors are
then compared using cosine similarity.

\subsection{Retrieval Performance Evaluation}

The main retrieval experiment evaluates the quality of the documents
selected by State Aware Retrieval. The selected SAR configuration is
fixed after validation and compared with the baseline methods.

Let $R_q$ be the set of relevant documents for question $q$, and let
$T_K(q)$ be the Top-$K$ retrieved documents.

\subsubsection{Recall@K}

\[
\mathrm{Recall@K}(q)
=
\frac{|T_K(q)\cap R_q|}
{|R_q|}
\]

Recall@K measures how much of the relevant evidence is retrieved.

\subsubsection{Precision@K}

\[
\mathrm{Precision@K}(q)
=
\frac{|T_K(q)\cap R_q|}
{K}
\]

Precision@K measures the proportion of retrieved documents that are
relevant.

\subsubsection{Hit@K}

\[
\mathrm{Hit@K}(q)
=
\begin{cases}
1, & T_K(q)\cap R_q\neq\emptyset,\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Hit@K measures whether at least one relevant document is retrieved.

\subsubsection{Mean Reciprocal Rank}

If $r_q$ is the rank of the first relevant document:

\[
\mathrm{RR}(q)=\frac{1}{r_q}
\]

\[
\mathrm{MRR}
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{q=1}^{N}
\mathrm{RR}(q)
\]

MRR measures how early the first relevant document appears.

\subsubsection{nDCG@K}

\[
\mathrm{DCG@K}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{K}
\frac{2^{rel_i}-1}
{\log_2(i+1)}
\]

\[
\mathrm{nDCG@K}
=
\frac{\mathrm{DCG@K}}
{\mathrm{IDCG@K}}
\]

where $rel_i$ is the relevance value at rank $i$ and
$\mathrm{IDCG@K}$ is the ideal DCG \cite{jarvelin2002cumulated}.

Only metrics supported by the ground-truth relevance information of a
dataset are reported.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Iterative State Analysis}

State Aware Retrieval is intended to change its retrieval behavior as
the evidence and information gap change. This experiment studies the
retrieval results across successive iterations.

Let $T_t$ be the documents retrieved at iteration $t$.

\subsubsection{Cumulative Recall}

\[
U_t
=
\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}T_i
\]

\[
\mathrm{CumulativeRecall}_t
=
\frac{|U_t\cap R_q|}
{|R_q|}
\]

This measures the relevant evidence collected up to iteration $t$.

\subsubsection{Marginal Recall Gain}

\[
\Delta R_t
=
\mathrm{CumulativeRecall}_t
-
\mathrm{CumulativeRecall}_{t-1}
\]

This measures the additional relevant evidence found at the current
iteration.

\subsubsection{New Document Rate}

\[
\mathrm{NewRate}_t
=
\frac{
\left|
T_t
\setminus
\bigcup_{i=1}^{t-1}T_i
\right|
}
{|T_t|}
\]

This measures the proportion of newly retrieved documents.

\subsubsection{Retrieval Overlap}

\[
J(T_t,T_{t-1})
=
\frac{|T_t\cap T_{t-1}|}
{|T_t\cup T_{t-1}|}
\]

This measures the overlap between successive retrieval sets.

These measures are used to determine whether the changing evidence and
information gap lead to different and complementary retrieval results.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Downstream RAG and Robustness Evaluation}

State Aware Retrieval is evaluated within the MIGRES framework to
measure its effect on the final answer. The same MIGRES configuration
and language model are used for SAR and the baseline retrieval methods.

\subsubsection{Answer Evaluation}

Final answer quality is measured using the official metrics of each
dataset where available.

Exact Match:

\[
\mathrm{EM}
=
\frac{
\#\text{exactly correct answers}
}
{N}
\]

Accuracy:

\[
\mathrm{Accuracy}
=
\frac{
\#\text{correct answers}
}
{N}
\]

For token-level F1:

\[
P
=
\frac{
|\mathrm{tokens}(A_p)\cap\mathrm{tokens}(A_g)|
}
{|\mathrm{tokens}(A_p)|}
\]

\[
R
=
\frac{
|\mathrm{tokens}(A_p)\cap\mathrm{tokens}(A_g)|
}
{|\mathrm{tokens}(A_g)|}
\]

\[
F1
=
\frac{2PR}{P+R}
\]

\subsubsection{RGB Robustness}

The Retrieval-Augmented Generation Benchmark (RGB) is used to test
robustness under four settings: noise robustness, negative rejection,
information integration, and counterfactual robustness
\cite{chen2024rgb}.

For noise robustness:

\[
\mathrm{Accuracy}(\rho)
=
\frac{
\#\text{correct answers at noise level }\rho
}
{N}
\]

For negative rejection:

\[
\mathrm{RejectionRate}
=
\frac{
\#\text{correctly rejected cases}
}
{
\#\text{negative cases}
}
\]

For counterfactual error detection:

\[
\mathrm{ED}
=
\frac{
\#\text{correctly detected counterfactual cases}
}
{
\#\text{counterfactual cases}
}
\]

For counterfactual correction:

\[
\mathrm{CR}
=
\frac{
\#\text{correctly corrected detected cases}
}
{
\#\text{detected error cases}
}
\]

The same evaluation settings are used for SAR and the selected baseline
methods.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Computational Cost Evaluation}

The computational cost of State Aware Retrieval is measured because the
method scores the complete document corpus at each retrieval iteration.

\subsubsection{Retrieval Latency}

Let $L_i$ be the retrieval time for question $i$.

\[
L_{\mathrm{avg}}
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_i
\]

\subsubsection{Documents Scored}

If the corpus contains $|\mathcal{D}|$ documents and the system performs
$T$ retrieval iterations:

\[
N_{\mathrm{scored}}
=
|\mathcal{D}|\times T
\]

\subsubsection{Similarity Calculations}

If each document is compared with one question, $m$ gaps, and $n$
evidence items:

\[
N_{\mathrm{similarity}}
=
|\mathcal{D}|(1+m+n)
\]

\subsubsection{Retrieval Overhead}

\[
\mathrm{Overhead(\%)}
=
\frac{
L_{\mathrm{SAR}}-L_{\mathrm{baseline}}
}
{
L_{\mathrm{baseline}}
}
\times100
\]

The experiment records retrieval time, total iteration time, embedding
time, number of documents scored, and memory usage.

\section{Experimental Environment and Reproducibility}

The project uses a Python-based environment for dataset processing,
document embedding, State Aware Retrieval, MIGRES integration, and
evaluation. The dataset versions, model versions, software packages,
hardware, and experiment settings are recorded for reproducibility.

The document corpus is embedded before retrieval and the embeddings are
stored for reuse. At the end of each retrieval iteration, the corpus is
checked for new or updated documents. New documents are embedded and
added to the stored collection, while updated documents are re-embedded.

The main experimental pipeline is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Document Corpus}
&\rightarrow \text{Document Embeddings}\\
&\rightarrow \text{MIGRES State}
\rightarrow \text{State Aware Retrieval}\\
&\rightarrow \text{Top-}K
\rightarrow \text{MIGRES State Update}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The scoring hyperparameters are selected using validation data, while
the retrieval settings are fixed according to the experimental design.
The selected configuration is frozen before the final test evaluation.

\section{Chapter Solution Summary}

This chapter presented State Aware Retrieval (SAR) as a direct retrieval
method within the MIGRES framework. SAR uses the current question,
information gaps, and collected evidence to guide document selection
from the complete document corpus.

The documents are represented using a common Sentence Transformer
embedding space. At each iteration, the documents are compared with the
current retrieval state, and a final score is used to select the
top-$K$ documents. The selected documents are then passed to MIGRES for
filtering, evidence extraction, and state update.

The proposed method does not use a separate BM25 candidate stage or a
fixed retrieval threshold. The final scoring formulation is developed
and evaluated during implementation. The experiments evaluate the
contribution of the state signals, compare SAR with existing retrieval
methods, measure retrieval and downstream RAG performance, study its
behaviour across iterations, and measure its computational cost.

\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{9. CONCLUSION}

\chaptertitle{9}{CONCLUSION}

\justifying

This methodology presents \textbf{State Aware Retrieval (SAR)} as a
direct retrieval method within the MIGRES framework. The proposed method
uses the current question, information gaps, and previously collected
evidence to guide document selection from the complete document corpus.
The selected documents are then passed to MIGRES for filtering, evidence
extraction, and further state updates.

The study will examine whether making retrieval aware of the current
information state can improve the quality and usefulness of retrieved
evidence during iterative retrieval. The contribution of each state
signal will be studied through ablation experiments, while comparisons
with established retrieval methods will measure the overall retrieval
performance of SAR. The effect on final RAG performance, behaviour across
iterations, robustness, and computational cost will also be evaluated.

Overall, this methodology provides a clear framework for testing whether
State Aware Retrieval can make iterative retrieval more focused on the
information that is relevant, still missing, and not already covered by
previous evidence. The main MIGRES framework remains unchanged, allowing
the study to directly examine the effect of the proposed retrieval
method. The final experimental results will determine the effectiveness
and practical value of SAR for iterative retrieval-augmented generation.
